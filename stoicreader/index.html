Privacy Policy — Stoic Reader

Last updated: 26 November 2026 (v1.0)

Stoic Reader (package com.bok.stoicreader) is a reader for public-domain English translations of Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus and Seneca, with an optional daily passage. This page explains what the app does with information.

Your data stays on your phone

Where you stopped reading in each chapter, which chapters you marked as read, the passages you saved, your text size and your daily-passage reminder setting are stored only on your device. There is no account, no sign-in and no server that receives any of it. The text of the books and of the passages you save is never included in the analytics described below.

If Android's own backup is turned on for your device, Android may include the app's data in your device backup so it can be restored on a new phone. That backup is handled by Android and your Google account, not by us.

Information the app sends

The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics to understand how it is used and to find crashes. What is sent is a short record of which feature was used:

  • That the home screen loaded, and that a book was opened, with which book and where it was opened from (for example the library, "continue reading", the daily passage or a notification).
  • That a chapter was marked as finished, with the number of chapters read as a range rather than an exact figure.
  • That a passage was saved, removed or shared — never the passage itself.
  • Whether the daily-passage reminder was turned on or off, the hour chosen, whether a reminder was shown or opened, the text size chosen, and whether notification permission was granted.
  • If something went wrong, a fixed error code.

The app does not send:

  • The text of anything you read, save or share.
  • Your name, email address, contacts or precise location.

Firebase itself adds some technical information to every record: an app instance identifier, your device model, Android version, app version, session timing, and an approximate region worked out from your IP address. Crashlytics adds a crash stack and device details when the app crashes.

Advertising

Every book, every chapter and every feature is free, with no account and nothing locked. The app is supported by ads served by Google AdMob: a banner at the bottom of the reading screen, which refreshes about once a minute while you read, and, occasionally, a full-screen ad. To serve and measure them, AdMob may use your device's advertising ID and an approximate location worked out from your IP address.

A full-screen ad can appear only after you tap Finished at the end of a chapter, and not every time. It never appears while you are reading, when you move to the next chapter, when you go back, or when you open or leave the app. There are no ads on the home, contents, saved passages or settings screens. If an ad fails to load or is blocked, the app works exactly the same.

Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and some US states), a consent or privacy choices form is shown using Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). You can reopen it at any time from Ad privacy choices in Settings. Where it is not required, the link is hidden.

Permissions

  • Notifications — only for the daily passage, which is off unless you turn it on. On Android 13 and later the app asks for permission only when you turn it on. You can say no; the reader works the same.
  • Internet and network state — for ads, analytics and crash reports. The books themselves are inside the app and work offline.

Firebase and the advertising SDK add a small number of background permissions that Android does not prompt you about, such as a wake lock, an attribution permission used by analytics, and the advertising ID permission used by AdMob.

The texts

The books are public-domain English translations: Meditations by Marcus Aurelius and the Enchiridion and Discourses of Epictetus, translated by George Long, and dialogues of Seneca translated by Aubrey Stewart. Sharing a passage uses Android's share sheet; the app does not see where you send it.

Children

The app is not directed at children. Beyond the anonymous usage records and the advertising identifier described above, it collects no information from anyone, including children.

Your choices

You can remove any saved passage in the app, or remove all data by clearing the app's storage or uninstalling it. You can reset or delete your advertising ID under Device Settings → Google → Ads, and opt out of personalised ads in your device settings. You can ask for the analytics records associated with your installation to be deleted by emailing the address below.

Changes to this policy

If a future version adds anything further that touches your information, this page will be updated before that version is published, and the date at the top will change.
