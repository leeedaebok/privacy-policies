Privacy Policy — Screen Test

Last updated: 8 September 2026

Screen Test (package com.bok.pixelcheck) is an Android app that checks a device's screen and reports what its hardware answers when asked directly. This page explains what the app does with information.

Information the app collects

The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics to understand how it is used and to find crashes. There is no account and no sign-in, and nothing you are shown in the app is sent anywhere. What is sent is a short record of which parts of the app were opened and whether a check came out clean.

Specifically, the app sends:

  • Which test you opened (screen colours, touch, uniformity, facts, sensors), and whether you finished it.
  • How many items disagreed, as a range rather than a number (for example "0", "1", "2_3"), and which kinds of item they were (for example "panel_size", "battery_health").
  • Whether a value could not be read on your device at all.
  • Whether you saved or sent a report, and whether that succeeded.
  • If something went wrong, a fixed error code and a shortened error message with any file path removed.

The app does not send:

  • The measured values themselves — not your resolution, refresh rate, sensor list, battery capacity or anything else the app shows you.
  • The contents of any report you produce.
  • Serial numbers, IMEI, advertising ID or account details.
  • The file name or location you chose when saving a report.

Firebase itself adds some technical information to every record: an app instance identifier, your device model, Android version, app version, session timing, and an approximate region worked out from your IP address. Crashlytics adds a crash stack and device details when the app crashes.

Permissions

The app asks you for no permissions — no location, contacts, camera, microphone or access to your files. Saving a report uses Android's own file picker, which needs no storage permission.

Firebase adds a small number of background permissions that Android does not prompt you about: internet access, network state, a wake lock, and an attribution permission used by analytics. The advertising ID permission that Firebase would normally add has been deliberately removed, because this app has no advertising.

Information the app reads on your device

To do its job, the app asks Android about the device it is running on: screen resolution and refresh rate, display modes, pixel density, HDR support, which sensors are present, the device model and Android version, the processor cores, battery capacity, and total memory.

These measurements are not sent anywhere. They are read while you are looking at them, and closing the app discards them. Only the summary described above — how many items disagreed, and of what kind — leaves the device.

Reports you create

The app can produce a plain text report of what it found. Nothing happens to that report unless you choose it:

  • Save as file asks Android to let you pick a location. The app writes the file where you point it and has no other access to your storage.
  • Send hands the text to whichever app you choose (email, a messaging app, and so on). What happens next is governed by that app's own privacy policy, not this one.

The report deliberately contains no serial number, no IMEI, no advertising identifier and no account details. It identifies the device only by model name and Android version, because the report is meant to be handed to a stranger such as a seller or a support agent.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone, including children.

Your choices

You can ask for the analytics records associated with your installation to be deleted by emailing the address below. Uninstalling the app also stops all further collection.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app adds anything further that touches your information — advertising, for example — this page will be updated before that version is published, and the date at the top will change.
